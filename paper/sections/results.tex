\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We present results up the hardness ladder: saturated stages first (what everyone
gets right), then the stages that separate methods, then the dual-oracle anchor,
the boundary map, and cost. Secondary axes (intervention, convergence,
detection) follow, in the order of the question hierarchy.

\paragraph{Rung 1: the saturated stage---and why saturation is a finding, not a headline.}
At $N{=}5$ full participation on clean stages, \textsc{Flirds},
\textsc{Flirds-1st}, the loss-heuristic, and exact Banzhaf all reproduce oracle
(b) exactly ($\rho = 1.000$; Table~\ref{tab:fidelity-llm}, left). This is
\emph{not} evidence of estimator quality alone: the underlying game is
near-additive there (measured non-additivity $\le 0.9\%$ of utility across
scales), so every reasonable index collapses to the same ranking, and the
singleton loss-heuristic matching the oracle is the cleanest \emph{additivity
probe}. We report rung 1 as a property of the stage. (With $N{=}5$ the
probability of a coincidental perfect ranking is $1/120$ per seed; the $N{=}10$
CNN track and \todo{the pending LLM $N{=}10$ oracle (E5)} carry the statistical
weight.)

\paragraph{Rung 2--3: partial participation separates methods.}
With partial cohorts (std20: $2/20$ per round) the completion/surrogate-based
baselines collapse (ShapleyFL $0.19$--$0.41$; ComFedSV down to $-0.137$ at 3B;
FedIF $0.16$--$0.48$) while \textsc{Flirds} stays at $0.999$--$1.000$
(Table~\ref{tab:fidelity-llm}, right). The dedicated probe at $5/50$
participation sharpens this: \textsc{Flirds}/\textsc{Flirds-1st} $+1.00$,
GTG $+0.98$, FedSV $+0.91$, versus \emph{negative} correlations for
ComFedSV ($-0.08$ to $-0.12$) and ShapleyFL ($-0.06$ to $-0.09$)
\verify{std50k5 probe; currently seed 0 only---extend to 3 seeds or annotate}.
Mechanistically, per-client participation count (not participation fraction) is
what matters; on the CNN track \textsc{Flirds-1st} itself degrades at low
per-client counts (label-flip, $k{=}0.2$: $+0.305$) where the second-order
\textsc{Flirds} holds ($+0.904$)---the first of three places the interaction
term earns its keep.

\paragraph{CNN control track ($N{=}10$, dual $2^{10}$ oracles).}
Pooled over 10 scenarios $\times$ 3 seeds: \textsc{Flirds} $0.919\pm.134$ vs (b)
(Banzhaf $0.989$, \textsc{Flirds-1st} $0.832$, loss-heuristic $0.860$, GTG
$0.569$, FedSV $0.401$, ShapleyFL $0.391$, Ripple $0.373$, ComFedSV $0.348$).
Cross-seed stability tracks the oracle's own: (b) self-stability $0.518$,
\textsc{Flirds} $0.547$---the estimator adds no seed-to-seed variance beyond
the target's intrinsic instability---while MC-based methods add sampling
variance and fall to $0.124$--$0.311$.

\paragraph{Value-level fidelity and axiom compliance.}
Rank correlation is not the whole ask: settlement-type uses consume the
\emph{values}. On the standard stages \textsc{Flirds} matches oracle (b) at
Pearson $1.000$ and near-zero distances (cosine distance $< 10^{-4}$) across
1B/3B/7B, and the efficiency identity
$\sum_k \hat\phi_k = \ell(w^0) - \ell(w^R)$ holds with zero telescoping gap in
our gate tests \verify{make\_fidelity.py Pearson/distance columns; phase-gate
telescoping check}. Axiom compliance separates methods where correlation
cannot: zero-update free-riders receive $\hat\phi = 0$ \emph{exactly}
(Proposition~\ref{prop:closed-form}; so do oracle (b), Banzhaf, and the
singleton heuristic), while the subset-renormalized methods pay them strictly
positive raw value ($0.0041$/$0.0053$ for GTG/FedSV)---the practical face of
the game gap of Proposition~\ref{prop:games}
\verify{master\_phi.csv raw $\phi$}.

\label{sec:results-boundary}

\emph{does the stage contain client-ranking signal in the first place?} We
answer with the oracle's own cross-seed self-consistency (Spearman of
$\phi^{(b)}$ across seeds):

Three consequences. \textbf{(i)} On IID-clean stages the exact oracle's own
ranking carries no stable signal across seeds: at 1B, $\rho \approx -0.37$ to
$-0.11$ on the LLM standard stages; across scales the self-consistency is
unstable in both directions ($-0.37$ to $+0.73$, the positive extreme at the
7B anchor being the noted exception \verify{canonical per-cell values in
\texttt{target\_stability.csv}: 1B anchor $-0.367$, 1B std20 $-0.114$, 3B
std20 $-0.243$, 3B anchor $+0.033$, 7B std20 $+0.164$, 7B anchor $+0.733$});
``fidelity $1.000$'' there means the estimator
faithfully reproduces per-run randomness---which is exactly the right behavior
for \emph{settlement of the realized run}, and exactly \emph{not} a stage where
any method can demonstrate downstream utility. \textbf{(ii)} Signal is created
by real inter-client differences---domain heterogeneity alone suffices
($+0.87$ with zero corruption)---not by training intensity: scaling learning
rate triples the \emph{magnitude} of $\phi$ while, in the seed-0 pilot, still
creating no cross-seed signal (cross-seed confirmation pending; the diagnosis
predicts $\rho \approx 0$) \verify{lr probe seed-0 only (E11)}. \textbf{(iii)} Intervention experiments on
IID-clean stages have ``do no harm'' as their correct answer; our
valuation-weighted arms indeed sit at parity there (MMLU/ROUGE within
$\pm0.003$ of vanilla), which we report as a designed sanity check, not a
failure.

\paragraph{Separating the noise floors.}
The cross-seed instability above is a property of the \emph{stage} (data
partition and trajectory randomness), not of validation sampling: within a
fixed run, bootstrap resampling of the validation set leaves the $\phi$
ranking essentially intact (self-$\rho$ $0.93$/$0.92$ at LoRA rank 16/64;
split-half $0.90$--$1.00$; extreme client pairs separated by
$\sim$$10\times$ the bootstrap SE), even though the inter-client $\phi$ spread
is only $\approx 1.1\times$ the per-client bootstrap SE
\verify{noise probe, anchor stage, seed 0 only}. Valuation on IID-clean stages
is thus \emph{precise but stage-limited}: the estimator resolves the realized
run's ordering through validation noise, and it is the ordering itself that
fails to replicate across runs.

\subsection{Adversarial boundary: clean-loss-preserving poison}
\label{sec:results-poison}

A backdoor that \emph{lowers} clean validation loss \citep{bagdasaryan2020}
defeats first-order valuation by construction: the first-order term rewards
descent along $-g_r$. Empirically, \textsc{Flirds-1st} is fully evaded (AUROC
$0.000$, 3 seeds, 1B silo), while the second-order term partially restores
separation (\textsc{Flirds} $0.917 \pm 0.118$; rank fidelity shows the same
repair, Spearman $0.000 \to 0.967 \pm .047$)---the interaction term detects
curvature-inconsistent updates that first-order alignment cannot. Value-level
fidelity collapses precisely there as well (Pearson $0.68$ at 1B; $-0.89$ at
3B, single seed): the $\gamma$-scaled replacement deltas exit the Taylor trust
region that Proposition~\ref{prop:residual} bounds \verify{poison Pearson
cells, R4 audit}. The defense is
scale- and stage-dependent: at 3B the second-order term is also evaded
\verify{single seed --- extend (E8) before claiming scale trend}, and on
IID stages evasion is more complete than non-IID ($0.00$ vs $0.92$).
Stage geometry can also work \emph{against} the attacker: under cross-device
dilution ($N{=}100$, cohort 10) the same attack is caught by both orders at
the extreme partition (AUROC $1.000$), and at $\alpha{=}0.5$ the second-order
term stabilizes detection where the first order becomes erratic
(\textsc{Flirds} $1.000$ vs \textsc{Flirds-1st} $0.670 \pm .467$; deployed ASR
itself is diluted to $\approx 0.5$) \verify{device100 poison cells}. The exact
oracle and the singleton heuristic \emph{do} flag the attacker
\pending{D-1: orientation framing --- ``evaded valuation'' vs ``honest answer
of a clean-loss utility''; two internal analyses currently disagree and the
paper must pick one and state it}.

\paragraph{Coalition axis.}
At cohort 10 (cross-device anchor), \textsc{Flirds} reproduces the exact
per-round oracle ranking ($\rho = 1.000$, $N{=}100$, $\alpha{=}0.5$) at
$157$s vs $\sim$$25{,}000$s for exact enumeration ($\approx 160\times$). At
cohort 2 (std20) the relation \emph{reverses}: exact enumeration costs
$2^2$/round and undercuts the HVP ($2{,}917$s vs $4{,}697$s;
\textsc{Flirds-1st} at $1{,}531$s remains cheaper). We therefore state the cost
claim conditionally: \textsc{Flirds}'s advantage is decisive exactly where
exact valuation is infeasible---large cohorts---which is the regime that
motivates estimation in the first place.

\paragraph{Granularity axis.}
Sample-level in-run valuation prices attribution as a multiple of training. On
the CNN control track---the one stage where both granularities run under one
accounting---sample-level Ripple's valuation pass costs $1{,}075$--$2{,}255$s
(MNIST) and $7{,}453$--$11{,}105$s (CIFAR-10) against a training run of
$80$--$94$s, i.e., $12$--$130\times$ the run it values; client-level
\textsc{Flirds} costs $0.6$--$5.8$s on the same stages ($0.08$--$0.4$s
first-order), $2$--$3$ orders of magnitude \emph{below} the run
\verify{overview \S3.5.2 CNN runtimes, 3-seed}. The asymmetry is structural,
not an implementation artifact: client-level scores consume only the $|P_r|$
deltas the server already holds (one HVP $+$ $|P_r|$ inner products), whereas
sample-level scores must touch every training example---computation that in FL
additionally sits on the wrong side of the client--server trust boundary.
Client granularity is therefore not a fallback from sample granularity but the
deployable unit of in-run valuation in FL; per-sample refinement \emph{within}
a consenting client is orthogonal, client-side future work
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}). Ripple is reported separately
($\sim$4{,}515s at 1B under a reduced-config port; its timer includes its own
required trajectory---the one accounting exception---and the port is
incomplete, so the number is accounting context only \verify{ripple-audit
2026-07}) \pending{D-6/D-7; end-to-end (training-inclusive) overhead column
pending the two-block restructure (shared-train wall-clock measured: silo/anchor
via unified-accounting 3-seed campaign; device-cohort trajectory time to be
reconstructed from driver logs)}.

\paragraph{Provenance of baseline costs.} All baseline runtimes are our
re-implementations measured under identical conditions (same frozen logs, loss
closure, GPU, fp32); the original papers report no comparable wall-clock (a
survey of seven finds cost stated as asymptotics or convergence-time curves, none
with our accounting), so these are not reproductions of published numbers but
new same-condition measurements---a stronger basis for comparison than the prior
practice. Two re-implementation choices are noted for fairness: FedSV adds TMC
truncation and subset caching (our optimization, not in the original Alg.\,2, so
its cost is if anything under-stated); ShapleyFL is run per-round exact; GTG's
original ``time'' is time-to-convergence rather than our fixed budget.
\verify{cost-comparison-methodology 2026-07 \S2.8, \S4 C4/C5}

\subsection{Secondary axes: intervention, convergence, detection}
\label{sec:results-secondary}

Per the question hierarchy, these validate the \emph{usefulness} of measured
contributions where signal exists; they are not the paper's headline.

\textbf{Intervention.} On stages with real signal, contribution-weighted
aggregation recovers what corruption destroys (CNN, $N{=}100$, threat-pooled):
grad-noise accuracy $0.499 \to 0.609$ flirds-weighted (ShapleyFL-weighted
reaches $0.645$---a faithful score does not make its naive consumption rule
optimal, and we say so), label-flip $0.583 \to 0.626$. In the
width/participation sweep the label-flip gain is $\approx$$+0.09$ accuracy for
the flirds arm---largest among arms---and invariant to $8\times$ model-width
changes, while \emph{every} clean cell stays at designed parity
($|\Delta| \le 0.006$) \verify{C2 pooled + probe cells}. Selection shows the
same conditional pattern: on the corruption-injected foundational stage the
$\phi$-top-$k$ selector recovers exactly the clean client set in every seed
and matches-or-beats both random selection and all-client training on final
validation loss \verify{phase1, $N{=}5$, small-stage scope}. On clean-IID LLM
stages all arms sit at parity, as the boundary map predicts. LLM
instruction-tuning gains show as ROUGE-L ($+6.7$ to $+12.8$pp over training on
the standard stage; $+1.6$ to $+5.6$pp on the anchor stage \verify{arm
metrics}), while MMLU is flat-to-negative for \emph{all} arms including
vanilla on the standard stage ($-0.8$ to $-1.4$pp; anchor-stage deltas are
$\approx 0$, and the 7B anchor vanilla is $+0.3$pp)---this SFT recipe teaches
format, not capability, so MMLU has $\approx$zero power as an intervention
metric here \pending{D-13 metric choice}.

\textbf{Convergence.} 7B std20 rounds-to-target drops from $184.7$ (vanilla) to
$\approx 153$ (flirds-weighted; seed spread overlaps---suggestive, not
conclusive). CNN corrupted stages show larger effects: grad-noise $27.2 \to
13.7$ (multiplicative) and $\to 6.3$ (replacement-weighted); free-rider
$41.2 \to 7.7$ (replacement-weighted, size-skew partition)
\verify{C2 rounds-to-target, threat-pooled}.

\textbf{Detection.} Detection is the hierarchy's last question, and the answer
is threat- and background-dependent---so we report it per threat, with the
oracle's own ceiling made explicit. On the $N{=}5$ non-IID silos, $\phi$-based
scores are competitive with dedicated detectors while also \emph{being} the
contribution measure: \textsc{Flirds} separates noisy and free-rider clients
at AUROC $1.000$ (3 seeds) where each dedicated detector has a home-turf hole
(FLDetector $0.750$ on noisy; FedDQC $0.750$ on free-riders; STD-DAGMM
$0.250$--$0.417$) \verify{silo5 detection cells}. The background matrix shows
the $\phi$ signal is background-independent (noisy/free-rider $1.00$ on both
IID and non-IID stages; so is FLTrust), while the data-quality detector FedDQC
erodes from $1.00$ (IID) to $0.92$ (non-IID) on noisy and fails free-riders
outright at cross-device scale ($0.14$--$0.57$, vs $\phi$-based $1.000$,
FLDetector $\approx 0.53$--$0.62$). The honest converse: on cross-device noisy,
FedDQC dominates (AUROC $1.0$) and \emph{all} loss-based scores sit at
$0.57$--$0.77$---\emph{including the exact oracle (b) at
$0.604 \pm 0.041$} \verify{overview \S3.4.2; a secondary analysis doc cites
0.660 --- reconcile from master\_metrics.csv}. We read this as intrinsic: a clean-validation-loss game is not a
corruption detector---detection is a byproduct of valuation and priced
accordingly---and the byproduct is strongest exactly where the axioms bind:
free-riders, where $\hat\phi = 0$ holds exactly
(Proposition~\ref{prop:closed-form}) for zero/random free-riders \todo{delta
free-rider (E7) is the acknowledged
stress case---run or scope the claim to zero/random}.
