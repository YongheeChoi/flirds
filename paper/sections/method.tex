\section{\textsc{Flirds}: a Closed-Form Estimator of the In-Run Game}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Estimator}
Taylor-expanding the round-$r$ utility \eqref{eq:inrun-utility} around $w^r$ to
second order and taking the exact Shapley value of the resulting quadratic
surrogate game yields a closed form (Proposition~\ref{prop:closed-form}); with
$g_r := \nabla \ell(w^r)$, $H_r := \nabla^2 \ell(w^r)$ (validation loss;
true Hessian, not Gauss--Newton), $\delta_k := \Delta w_k^r$, and
$\Delta W_r := \sum_{j \in P_r} p_j^r\, \delta_j$:
\begin{equation}
\hat\phi_k^{(r)}
\;=\;
-\,p_k^r\, \big\langle g_r,\, \delta_k \big\rangle
\;-\; \tfrac{1}{2}\, p_k^r\, \big\langle \delta_k,\; H_r\, \Delta W_r \big\rangle,
\qquad
\hat\phi_k = \sum_{r\,:\,k\in P_r} \hat\phi_k^{(r)}.
\label{eq:flirds}
\end{equation}
The first term is the aligned loss-reduction of client $k$'s own update; the
second is the \emph{client-interaction} term, capturing how $k$'s update composes
with the aggregate through validation curvature. The entire round costs
\textbf{one} Hessian--vector product $u_r := H_r \Delta W_r$ (forward-mode HVP;
we never form or invert $H_r$) plus $|P_r|$ inner products $\langle \delta_k,
u_r\rangle$---independent of $2^{|P_r|}$, and cheap in LoRA dimension.
\textsc{Flirds-1st} denotes the self-ablation with the second-order term removed.

\begin{proposition}[Per-round decomposition]\label{prop:decomp}
On the frozen trajectory, the $N$-player game $\sum_r u_r$ satisfies the
null-player property round-wise, and its Shapley value decomposes exactly into
the sum of per-round $|P_r|$-player Shapley values. (Numerically: per-round
enumeration matches direct $2^N$ enumeration to $3.9\times 10^{-7}$, the fp32
forward-noise floor, in a GPT-2 smoke test; a separate deterministic CNN
measurement reaches $3\times 10^{-16}$.)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Closed form; free-rider zero; efficiency]\label{prop:closed-form}
For the quadratic surrogate of $u_r$, the exact Shapley value equals
\eqref{eq:flirds} (the closed form matches our implementation to
$5.8\times 10^{-12}$ and the exact $2^N$ Shapley value of the surrogate game to
$7.6\times 10^{-12}$). Consequently a client with $\delta_k = 0$ receives exactly
$\hat\phi_k = 0$, algebraically; and the values satisfy efficiency for the
surrogate game.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Taylor residual]\label{prop:residual}
Per round, $|u_r(S) - \hat u_r(S)| \le \frac{M_3}{6}\|\Delta_S\|^3$ for the
second-order surrogate and $\frac{M_2}{2}\|\Delta_S\|^2$ at first order (both
at utility level; Shapley-level bounds propagate with constants given in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), accumulating linearly in $R$. The constants are supremum bounds, not
estimated quantities; we therefore also measure the residual empirically.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed weights vs.\ subset-renormalization]\label{prop:games}
The in-run oracle and \textsc{Flirds} use the run's fixed weights $p_k^r$
\eqref{eq:inrun-utility}. Several baselines (GTG, FedSV) instead evaluate
coalitions with weights renormalized within $S$
($p_k^S = n_k / \sum_{j\in S} n_j$)---a \emph{different} cooperative game.\footnote{The
renormalized-coalition semantics of these baselines is verified in our
reimplementations (weights renormalized within $S$ in the shared coalition
evaluator); see Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.} Under
equal client sizes, full participation, and first-order utilities the two games
have identical rankings; outside that regime they \emph{can} provably
diverge---explicit rank-flip counterexamples exist at $n$-ratio $3{:}1$, under
partial participation, and under second-order curvature amplification (the
last even with equal sizes and full participation). Fixed weights are moreover forced
for the closed form: renormalized utilities break the sum-form structure that
collapses the round to one HVP.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[What this does and does not claim]
Proposition~\ref{prop:games} implies our fidelity tables compare methods that
target \emph{different games} wherever sizes are unequal, participation is
partial, or second-order curvature is non-negligible. We therefore (i) report each method against oracle (b) as the
\emph{deployment-relevant} question (``does the score track the realized run's
exact value?''), and (ii) \todo{report the own-game decomposition and the

game mismatch is measured, not blamed on the estimator quality of baselines.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Scope: path dependence and LoRA coordinates]\label{prop:scope}
Oracle (b) is a functional of the realized trajectory (a sequence function); we
inherit, and state, the open axiomatization of trajectory-specific values
\citep{wang2024irds}. The relationship between oracles (a) and (b) is an
empirical question, and reported as such. All statements hold in LoRA-factor
coordinates; we make no claim in full-weight space.
\end{proposition}
