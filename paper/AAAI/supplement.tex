\section{Proofs and Formal Statements}
\label{app:proofs}

This appendix does not re-prove results that are already in IRDS: the
per-round decomposition and the closed-form derivation are direct readings of
IRDS's step-level results, so we give statements and correspondences only
(Supplementary Sections~\ref{app:game} and~\ref{app:closed}); proofs are
attached to what IRDS does not formalize --- the residual (Supplementary
Section~\ref{app:residual}) and the FL-specific items (Supplementary
Sections~\ref{app:bridge}--\ref{app:lora}). The closed form and the per-round
decomposition are also verified numerically in code; the verified values and
the reproduction sources are given in Supplementary
Section~\ref{app:verify} and the submitted code package.

Notation: in this appendix, \emph{retraining reference} denotes the
retraining-based Shapley and \emph{in-run reference} the in-run Shapley
defined in the main paper's ``The Federated Round Game'' section.
Correspondence with the main paper: the first and third properties in its

Section~\ref{app:game}, and the second in Supplementary

\ref{app:choice}, and~\ref{app:lora}.

\subsection{System Model and Assumptions}
\label{app:model}

We assume synchronous FedAvg: the participants $P_r$ of round $r$ start
local training from the same initial state $w^r$ (submitting the model
differences $\delta_k^r$), and the server aggregates with the stateless
weighted sum $w^{r+1} = w^r + \sum_{k \in P_r} p_k^r \delta_k^r$,
$p_k^r = n_k / \sum_{j \in P_r} n_j$ (the weight denominator runs over all of
$P_r$ and is fixed so as not to depend on the coalition $S$). The local
optimizer is plain SGD without momentum at a constant learning rate, created
anew each round (stateless clients). This is the configuration of the
experimental stage; which of these assumptions is actually load-bearing is
separated out in Supplementary Section~\ref{app:state}. The frozen log is
$[(w^r, \{(\delta_k^r, n_k)\}_{k \in P_r})]_{r<R}$. Notation:
$g_r = \nabla\lval(w^r)$, $H_r = \nabla^2\lval(w^r)$ (the true Hessian, not
GGN/Fisher), $a_k^r = p_k^r \delta_k^r$,
$\Delta_S^r = \sum_{k \in S \cap P_r} a_k^r$, and
$\Delta W_r = \Delta_{P_r}^r$. The sign convention matches the main text:
$\phi$ attributes the \emph{decrease} of the validation loss (beneficial
client $\Rightarrow \phi_k > 0$).

\subsection{The Game and Two Consequences}
\label{app:game}

The round subgame is
$u_r(S) := \lval(w^r) - \lval(w^r + \Delta_S^r)$, the total game is
$U^{\mathrm{in}}(S) := \sum_r u_r(S)$, and the in-run reference is the
Shapley value of this game computed without approximation. The round
surrogate is
$\hat u_r(S) := -\langle g_r, \Delta_S^r \rangle
- \tfrac12 \langle \Delta_S^r, H_r \Delta_S^r \rangle$
(the first-order-only version is $\hat u_r^{(1)}$). \emph{The game the
estimator approximates is exactly $U^{\mathrm{in}}$.} What Flirds computes
exactly is the Shapley value of $\hat U^{\mathrm{in}} := \sum_r \hat u_r$:
same log, same $\lval$, same $S$-independent weights, same expansion point,
and the only difference is the per-round second-order Taylor truncation. The
retraining reference is the game retrained from scratch with $S$ only, in
which the denominator is renormalized to $\sum_{j \in S} n_j$ and the entire
trajectory depends on $S$ --- a \emph{different game} (the relation between
the two references is an empirical question, not a theoretical one;
Supplementary Section~\ref{app:choice}).

Two properties follow immediately from this game, both round-level readings
of IRDS's step-level arguments. \textbf{(i) Telescoping and efficiency}: if
$S \supseteq P_r$ then $u_r(S) = \lval(w^r) - \lval(w^{r+1})$, so summing
over rounds gives
$U^{\mathrm{in}}([N]) = \lval(w^0) - \lval(w^R)$, and combined with Shapley
efficiency the contributions of all clients sum, without approximation, to
the validation-loss decrease of the whole run. What makes the grand-coalition
perturbation coincide with the realized step in FL is server statelessness
(Supplementary Section~\ref{app:state}), where the consequences of breaking
it are also treated. \textbf{(ii) Per-round decomposition}: since $u_r$ is a
function of $S \cap P_r$ only, the null-player argument (a standard result of
cooperative game theory, which IRDS also invokes at step level) gives
$\phi_k(U^{\mathrm{in}}) = \sum_{r:\,k \in P_r} \phi_k^{P_r}(u_r|_{P_r})$
--- zero contribution in non-participating rounds is a consequence, not a
definition.

\subsection{Closed Form and the Zero-Update Zero}
\label{app:closed}

For $k \in P_r$ the estimator has the closed form stated in the main paper:
\[
\phi_k(\hat u_r)
= -\,p_k^r \langle g_r, \delta_k^r \rangle
- \tfrac12\, p_k^r \langle \delta_k^r,\, H_r \Delta W_r \rangle.
\]
The derivation is that of Theorem~4 of IRDS, with the single substitution of
the weighted update $a_k^r = p_k^r \delta_k^r$ for the sample-gradient term
$-\eta_t \nabla\ell_z$ (the structure in which each pairwise interaction
$q_{ij} = \langle a_i^r, H_r a_j^r \rangle$ is split half-and-half between
its two parties is also unchanged; Supplementary Section~\ref{app:bridge}
reuses it). The common right-hand vector $H_r \Delta W_r$ is shared by all
clients, so the round closes with \emph{one} HVP, and
$\sum_k \phi_k(\hat u_r) = \hat u_r(P_r)$ (efficiency). \emph{The zero-update
zero has different grounds on the two sides}: on the estimator side,
$\delta_k^r = 0$ makes the value an inner product with a zero tensor and
hence algebraically exactly zero (independent of numerical determinism),
whereas on the in-run-reference game side the zero presupposes
bit-determinism of the forward pass (established with cuDNN-deterministic
settings for the CNN track; conditional for the LLM track) --- the

\subsection{Taylor Residual and Error Propagation}
\label{app:residual}

Under $\lval \in C^3$ and local constants $M_2^r, M_3^r$,
$|u_r(S) - \hat u_r(S)| \le \tfrac{M_3^r}{6}\|\Delta_S^r\|^3$, and
$\tfrac{M_2^r}{2}\|\Delta_S^r\|^2$ at first order. \textbf{Error
propagation}: the difference between the Shapley values of two games is
bounded by twice the game difference
($|\phi_i(v) - \phi_i(v')| \le 2\|v - v'\|_\infty$). Combining,
$|\phi_k(U^{\mathrm{in}}) - \hat\phi_k|
\le \sum_r \tfrac{M_3^r}{3}\max_{S \subseteq P_r}\|\Delta_S^r\|^3$
(for the first-order estimator the leading coefficient is $M_2^r$; the
analogous $M_2^r/3$ would be an invalid bound). \textbf{Status}: this is an
upper bound, its constants are hard to estimate tightly for large models,
and it accumulates linearly in $R$ --- the role of this bound in this paper
stops at establishing the order (scaling law) of the residual, the same
theory-only status as the main paper's approximation-error discussion. We
also make explicit that the per-round displacement is an accumulation of
several local steps, hence a structurally weaker guarantee than IRDS's
per-step one. Moreover the bound is conditional on the $C^3$ assumption: the
LLM track in LoRA-factor coordinates satisfies it automatically
(Supplementary Section~\ref{app:lora}), but the ReLU CNN track does not ---
fidelity on CNN is claimed only as the empirical result of Section~5.2, never
as a consequence of this bound (the scope remark in the main paper points
here).

\subsection{The Per-Sample-to-Per-Client Bridge}
\label{app:bridge}

\textbf{(i) The role of leaving the weights untouched in the additive closed
form}: the renormalized displacement $\widetilde\Delta_S$ cannot be
expressed as a sum over a coalition-independent family of vectors (equal-$n$
two-client counterexample: the singletons force $b_k = \delta_k$ while the
pair forces $\tfrac12(\delta_1 + \delta_2)$). Leaving the coefficients as-is
is therefore not an assumption required of every FL valuation, but the
structural condition for obtaining this paper's one-HVP closed form as a sum
of coalition-independent displacements while preserving the realized grand
coalition. \textbf{(ii) Merge-consistency}: in a 2-additive game, the
Shapley value after merging a block of players equals exactly the sum of the
within-block Shapley values (every pairwise interaction is always split
half-and-half between its two parties). \textbf{(iii) Corollary}: in the
one-step, full-batch, mean-CE limit, the per-client game is a block merge of
the per-sample game, and a client's value equals the sum of its samples'
values (exactly). \textbf{Scope}: this cancellation holds only when the
reduction denominator of the local loss equals the FedAvg aggregation
weight. Mean-CE CNN (number of images) satisfies this, but token-mean CE in
LLMs does not (denominator = token count, weight = sequence count), so we do
not ground LLM valuation on sums of per-sample values. \textbf{(iv)
Third-order failure counterexample}: merging the three-player unanimity game
into blocks $\{1,2\},\{3\}$ gives block sum $\tfrac23 \ne$ merged Shapley
$\tfrac12$. Beyond the second-order surrogate the two units differ in
principle (the size of the discrepancy is bounded by the third-order
residual). For the same reason, the round game that bundles several local
steps is a \emph{different game} from IRDS's per-step game --- a
definitional choice arising because the server does not observe steps, not
an approximation of the per-step game; its price appears as the residual of
Supplementary Section~\ref{app:residual}.

\subsection{Choice of Game and Path Dependence}
\label{app:choice}

In the equal-$n$, first-order limit, the game with weights left as-is and
the renormalized game have identical per-round rankings (the renormalized
Shapley is an affine map of $b_k$ with a common positive slope), but this
reassurance is narrow: (1) in an unequal-$n$ counterexample
($n_1{:}n_2 = 3{:}1$, $b = (1,2)$) the ranking flips even at first order;
(2) under partial participation the round-sum lift breaks (three-client,
two-round counterexample); (3) even in the equal-$n$, exactly additive game,
the $c_S^2$ amplification of singleton curvature flips rankings. The
near-additivity identity
$\hat u_r(P_r) - \sum_k \hat u_r(\{k\}) = \sum_{i<j} q_{ij}$ says only that
semivalues of the \emph{same} game collapse together, not that two
\emph{different} games give the same answer. \textbf{Path dependence}: the
algebra of summing rounds is exact (linearity), but path dependence in the
sense that the expansion point $w^r$ is a function of the realized
trajectory is inherent to the in-run-reference game. Even at $R = 1$ (no
subsequent trajectory, so path dependence is exactly zero), counterexample
(1) above flips the ranking between the retraining reference and the in-run
reference, so their agreement does not follow from trajectory stability
alone and additionally requires equal-$n$/near-additive conditions. The
axiomatization of trajectory-specific utilities is an open problem stated by
IRDS and inherited by this paper (axiom claims are confined to the game on
the fixed trajectory).

\subsection{Scoping of State}
\label{app:state}

Whether or not the local optimizer is stateful, the game whose players are
the stored realized $\delta$ and its closed form remain well defined. The
genuinely load-bearing condition is the \emph{stateless additivity of server
aggregation}: with server momentum (FedAvgM) the grand-coalition
perturbation no longer coincides with the realized step, telescoping
(Supplementary Section~\ref{app:game}) breaks, and the game loses its
semantics as an additive decomposition of the realized run. A local optimizer
with cross-round state destroys not the score formula but the
per-sample-to-per-client bridge of Supplementary
Section~\ref{app:bridge} (Sections~5.1 and~6.2).

\subsection{LoRA Coordinates}
\label{app:lora}

The game, the estimator, and the in-run reference are all defined and
computed in the same LoRA-factor coordinates $\theta$, so the properties of
Supplementary Sections~\ref{app:game}--\ref{app:state} and the residual bound
hold verbatim under the substitution
$\lval \to \tilde\ell_{\mathrm{val}}(\theta)$, and the subspace restriction
introduces no additional error (the LoRA map is polynomial, so
$\tilde\ell_{\mathrm{val}} \in C^3$ automatically). The game value itself is
defined by model evaluations only and is therefore coordinate-free. The
substantive reasons for withholding a full-weight reinterpretation are two:
(1) aggregation is linear in the factors, so the additive structure
$\Delta_S^r = \sum_k a_k^r$ is a fact of $\theta$-space --- by the
bilinearity of $w = W_0 + BA$, the full-weight displacement of a coalition
contains cross-client products
$p_j^r p_k^r\, \Delta B_j \Delta A_k$ and is in general not expressible as a
sum of coalition-independent per-client vectors (the same type of
inexpressibility as Supplementary Section~\ref{app:bridge}(i)); (2) in the
Hessian chain rule
$\nabla_\theta^2 \tilde\ell_{\mathrm{val}}
= J^\top \nabla_w^2 \lval\, J
+ \sum_i \partial_i \lval\, \nabla_\theta^2 w_i$
(with $J = \partial w / \partial\theta$), the second (map-curvature) term is
precisely what accounts for the cross products above, so the second-order
surrogate is coordinate-dependent, and the $\theta$-Hessian must not be read
as the pullback $J^\top \nabla_w^2 \lval\, J$ of the weight Hessian.

\subsection{Algebraic Verification (GPT-2 Smoke Test)}
\label{app:verify}

On GPT-2 ($N{=}5$, $R{=}3$, fp32): closed form vs.\ estimator, maximum
absolute difference $5.8 \times 10^{-12}$; exhaustive $2^5$ Shapley of the
$\hat u^{(2)}$ game vs.\ closed form, $7.6 \times 10^{-12}$; per-round
decomposition vs.\ exhaustive $2^N$ in-run reference,
$3.9 \times 10^{-7}$ (the fp32 forward noise floor); telescoping residual
$\le 9.5 \times 10^{-7}$; Hessian symmetry $\le 1.8 \times 10^{-11}$.
