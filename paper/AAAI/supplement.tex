\section{Proofs and Formal Statements}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{System Model and Assumptions}
\label{app:model}

We assume synchronous FedAvg. The participants $P_r$ of round $r$ all start
local training from the same initial state $w^r$ and submit model differences
$\delta_k^r$, which the server aggregates as
$w^{r+1} = w^r + \sum_{k \in P_r} p_k^r \delta_k^r$ with
$p_k^r = n_k / \sum_{j \in P_r} n_j$. The weights are held fixed
independently of the coalition $S$. The frozen log is
$[(w^r, \{(\delta_k^r, n_k)\}_{k \in P_r})]_{r<R}$, and we write
$g_r = \nabla\lval(w^r)$, $H_r = \nabla^2\lval(w^r)$ (the true Hessian),
$a_k^r = p_k^r \delta_k^r$, $\Delta_S^r = \sum_{k \in S \cap P_r} a_k^r$, and
$\Delta W_r = \Delta_{P_r}^r$. Since $\phi$ attributes the decrease of the
validation loss, a beneficial client receives $\phi_k > 0$.

The system condition that is actually required is not statelessness of the
local optimizer but stateless additivity of the server aggregation. Once
server state intervenes between rounds, as under server momentum or Adam, the
grand-coalition perturbation no longer coincides with the realized step and
the telescoping below fails.

\subsection{The Round Game and Its Decomposition}
\label{app:game}

Let the round subgame and the whole-run game be
$u_r(S) := \lval(w^r) - \lval(w^r + \Delta_S^r)$ and
$U^{\mathrm{in}} := \sum_r u_r$. The in-run Shapley is the Shapley value of
$U^{\mathrm{in}}$, and Flirds computes exactly the Shapley value of
$\hat U^{\mathrm{in}}$, the sum of
$\hat u_r(S) := -\langle g_r, \Delta_S^r\rangle
- \tfrac12\langle \Delta_S^r, H_r \Delta_S^r\rangle$
(the first-order-only version is $\hat u_r^{(1)}$). The difference between
the two games is the per-round Taylor truncation and nothing else.

The players are neither individual samples nor local steps but the
accumulated update a client submits in one round. This is therefore a
different game from the step game of IRDS, and the price of using the larger
round displacement as the expansion displacement is charged to the residual
of Supplementary Section~\ref{app:residual}. The expansion point $w^r$ also
depends on the realized trajectory, so the Shapley-axiom claims of the main
paper are confined to this frozen in-run game. The retraining-based Shapley,
which retrains from scratch with $S$ alone and thereby changes the weights
and the trajectory, is a different game, and the relation between the two is
evaluated empirically in Section~5.2 of the main paper.

\begin{lemma}[null-player reduction]
\label{lem:null}
If $v(\emptyset) = 0$ and $v(S) = v(S \cap T)$ for every $S$, then
$\phi_i(v) = 0$ for $i \notin T$ and $\phi_i(v) = \phi_i^T(v|_T)$ for
$i \in T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every marginal of $i \notin T$ is zero. The marginals of $i \in T$ depend
only on $S \cap T$, and a uniform permutation of the full player set induces
a uniform relative order on $T$, so the value coincides with the permutation
form of the Shapley value of the restricted game.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[round decomposition and efficiency]
\label{prop:decomp}
If $S \supseteq P_r$ then $u_r(S) = \lval(w^r) - \lval(w^{r+1})$, so
$U^{\mathrm{in}}([N]) = \lval(w^0) - \lval(w^R)$. By Shapley efficiency the
values of all clients therefore sum to the validation-loss decrease of the
whole run. Moreover $u_r(S) = u_r(S \cap P_r)$, so linearity and
Lemma~\ref{lem:null} give
\[
\phi_k(U^{\mathrm{in}}) = \sum_{r:\,k \in P_r} \phi_k^{P_r}(u_r|_{P_r}).
\]
\end{proposition}

The zero value in non-participating rounds is a consequence of this
decomposition. The decomposition itself does not depend on holding the
weights fixed; what the fixed weights are needed for is the
coalition-independent additive structure and the closed form below.

\subsection{Closed Form and Fixed Weights}
\label{app:closed}

\begin{theorem}[closed form]
\label{thm:closed}
If $\lval$ is twice differentiable at $w^r$ and $H_r$ is symmetric, then for
$k \in P_r$
\[
\phi_k(\hat u_r)
= -p_k^r \langle g_r, \delta_k^r\rangle
- \tfrac12 p_k^r \langle \delta_k^r, H_r \Delta W_r\rangle,
\]
which is Eq.~(6) of the main paper.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Writing $q_{ij} := \langle a_i^r, H_r a_j^r\rangle$,
\[
\hat u_r(S)
= \sum_{k \in S}\Big(-\langle g_r, a_k^r\rangle - \tfrac12 q_{kk}\Big)
- \sum_{\{i,j\} \subseteq S,\; i \ne j} q_{ij}.
\]
The first term is an additive game, and by Shapley symmetry and efficiency
each two-party interaction in the second term is split half and half between
its two parties. Hence
$\phi_k(\hat u_r) = -\langle g_r, a_k^r\rangle
- \tfrac12 \sum_{j \in P_r} q_{kj}
= -\langle g_r, a_k^r\rangle - \tfrac12 \langle a_k^r, H_r \Delta W_r\rangle$.
\end{proof}

In the last equality the common vector $H_r \Delta W_r$ is shared by every
client, so one HVP per round suffices. The expression holds unchanged at
$|P_r| = 1$, and $\sum_k \phi_k(\hat u_r) = \hat u_r(P_r)$ follows
immediately from the decomposition above.

Renormalizing the coefficients of the remaining clients within a partial
coalition gives
$\widetilde\Delta_S
= \sum_{k \in S} \frac{n_k}{\sum_{j \in S} n_j}\, \delta_k^r$,
which in general is not expressible as a sum of coalition-independent vectors
$b_k$. Even for two clients with equal $n$, the singletons force
$b_k = \delta_k$ while the pair gives
$\widetilde\Delta_{\{1,2\}} = \tfrac12(\delta_1 + \delta_2) \ne b_1 + b_2$.
Holding the weights fixed is therefore not a condition on every FL valuation
but the condition for this additive structure and for the one-HVP closed
form. The semantics differ as well: ``had only these terms been reflected in
the realized round'' against ``had only these clients taken part''.

\textbf{The zero-update zero.} If $\delta_k^r = 0$ the estimator takes an
inner product with a zero tensor, so its value is algebraically exactly zero
irrespective of numerical determinism. The exact zero of the in-run Shapley
game, by contrast, presupposes bit-determinism of the forward pass
(established for the CNN track, conditional for the LLM track). The
zero-semantics of Section~5.3 of the main paper and of Supplementary
Section~\ref{app:intervention} rest on the former.

\subsection{Taylor Residual and Error Propagation}
\label{app:residual}

Let $\mathcal U_r$ be an open convex set containing every coalition segment
$\{w^r + t\Delta_S^r : t \in [0,1],\, S \subseteq P_r\}$ and put
\[
M_2^r := \sup_{w \in \mathcal U_r}\|\nabla^2\lval(w)\|_{\mathrm{op}}, \quad
D_r := \max_{S \subseteq P_r}\|\Delta_S^r\|,
\]
\[
M_3^r := \sup_{w \in \mathcal U_r,\, \|v\| = 1}
|\nabla^3\lval(w)[v,v,v]|.
\]
The norms are the Euclidean norms of the relevant training coordinates.

\begin{proposition}[utility residual]
\label{prop:residual}
If $\lval \in C^3(\mathcal U_r)$ then
\[
|u_r(S) - \hat u_r(S)| \le \tfrac{M_3^r}{6}\|\Delta_S^r\|^3, \qquad
|u_r(S) - \hat u_r^{(1)}(S)| \le \tfrac{M_2^r}{2}\|\Delta_S^r\|^2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the integral form of the Taylor residual to
$h(t) = \lval(w^r + t\Delta_S^r)$ and bound
$|h''(t)| \le M_2^r\|\Delta_S^r\|^2$ and
$|h'''(t)| \le M_3^r\|\Delta_S^r\|^3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Shapley error propagation]
\label{lem:prop}
If $v(\emptyset) = v'(\emptyset) = 0$ then
$|\phi_i(v) - \phi_i(v')| \le 2\max_S |v(S) - v'(S)|$.
\end{lemma}

Each marginal of the difference game is bounded by the right-hand side, and a
Shapley value is a convex combination of those marginals.

\begin{corollary}[player-level bound]
\label{cor:player}
Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:residual} and Lemma~\ref{lem:prop} to the
round decomposition of Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp},
\[
|\phi_k(U^{\mathrm{in}}) - \hat\phi_k|
\le \sum_{r:\,k \in P_r} \tfrac{M_3^r}{3} D_r^3, \qquad
|\phi_k(U^{\mathrm{in}}) - \hat\phi_k^{(1)}|
\le \sum_{r:\,k \in P_r} M_2^r D_r^2.
\]
\end{corollary}

This bound is used only to establish the order of the residual. Its constants
are hard to estimate tightly for large models and they accumulate across
rounds, and $D_r$ is the largest coalition displacement rather than the
realized aggregate displacement. It is also conditional on the $C^3$
assumption, so it applies to the LLM track with its smooth activations
(Supplementary Section~\ref{app:lora}), whereas fidelity on the ReLU CNN
track is claimed only from the empirical results of Section~5.2 of the main
paper.

\subsection{LoRA Coordinates}
\label{app:lora}

The game, the estimator, and the in-run Shapley of the LLM track are all
defined and computed in the same LoRA-factor coordinates $\theta$, so
Supplementary Sections~\ref{app:game}--\ref{app:residual} hold under the
substitution $\lval \to \tilde\ell_{\mathrm{val}}(\theta)$. The composition of
smooth activations with the polynomial map $\theta \mapsto W_0 + BA$ is
$C^3$, so the assumption of Supplementary Section~\ref{app:residual} is
preserved as well.

The second-order surrogate is, however, coordinate-dependent. Because $BA$ is
bilinear, the additive structure of the factor updates cannot in general be
reinterpreted as per-client additive displacements in full-weight space, and
the curvature of the LoRA map is itself part of the Hessian. The closed form
and the residual claims of this paper are therefore confined to the
LoRA-factor coordinates.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{app:protocol}

This appendix collects the details needed to rerun the experiments of
Section~5 of the main paper and to regenerate the numbers in the tables. The
skeleton of each setting, that is the model and dataset, the partition, the
number of clients, the participation rate, the number of rounds, and the
corruption fraction, is already given in Section~5.1 of the main paper and in
the caption of each results table, so it is not repeated here; what is added
is the training configuration and the data split laid on top of it.
Supplementary Section~\ref{app:train} gives the training configuration,
Supplementary Section~\ref{app:threats} the implementation of the threats,
and Supplementary Section~\ref{app:data} the data distribution and the
evaluation sets.

\subsection{Training Configuration}
\label{app:train}

The two tracks share the local optimizer: plain SGD without momentum at a
constant learning rate, $10^{-3}$ for the LLM track and $0.01$ for the CNN
track. The local workload per round is 10 steps at batch 16 for the LLM track
and 5 epochs at batch 64 for the CNN track. The LLM track trains and
exchanges only LoRA ($r{=}16$, $\alpha{=}32$) factors rather than the full
weights. The maximum sequence length is 512 in the GSM8K main setting and the
alpaca setting and 768 in the five-domain non-IID setting, and a warmup of 2
rounds is used in the five-domain setting only. The CNN models are FedSVCNN
for CIFAR-10 and LeNet5 for MNIST. Two settings whose scale is not written in
a results-table caption are the five-domain non-IID setting ($N{=}5$, full
participation, $R{=}10$) and the alpaca anchor ($N{=}5$, full participation,
$R{=}30$). Every setting is trained and scored in fp32, and the original
configuration values of each cell are kept in the config of the corresponding
artifact.

\subsection{Implementation of the Threats}
\label{app:threats}

\textbf{clean} is the uncorrupted control cell and the anchor for the other
four threats. The variance of the target values across clients is small
there, so a low correlation in this cell is not read as a failure of any
method.

\textbf{answer-swap} (LLM) permutes, in a prescribed fraction of a client's
data, the response (the derivation and the final answer) with that of another
question held by the same client. The data format stays intact and only the
correspondence between question and response is broken, which is a realistic
mislabeling. The fraction is $0.7$ in the LLM main setting and $1.0$ in the
five-domain non-IID setting.

\textbf{zero-update free-rider} submits $\Delta = 0$ without local training.

\textbf{gradient noise} (CNN) adds Gaussian noise to the submitted update
after normal local training.

\textbf{label-flip} (CNN) replaces a fraction $0.7$ of the labels held by a
corrupted client with random labels. The set of corrupted clients is drawn
per client as an independent Bernoulli($\rho{=}0.4$), following the noise
model $(\rho, \tau)$ of FedCorr \citep{xu2022fedcorr} exactly as in its
official implementation, so
the number of corrupted clients varies with the seed and the tables report
the realized number rather than the nominal $\rho$.

\subsection{Data Distribution and Evaluation Sets}
\label{app:data}

GSM8K distributes the 7{,}473 official training questions IID and uniformly
over the clients, giving 149 questions per client. The server validation set
is 200 questions taken from the 1{,}319 official test questions, and
performance is measured by exact match on the remaining 1{,}119. The
separation between training and evaluation data is the official split, and
the validation and evaluation sets are drawn from the same test pool, so they
share a distribution and do not overlap in questions. In the five-domain
non-IID setting one client holds one domain, with 200 training, 20
validation, and 40 test examples per domain. The alpaca setting splits
alpaca-gpt4 20k IID and holds out 200 validation and 1{,}000 test examples.

The CNN track uses two partitions. The \texttt{iid} partition is a uniform
random split and \texttt{Dirichlet($\alpha{=}1$)} is a split in which the
labels and the sizes are skewed together; the seed used for the partition is
the run seed. The $N{=}10$ grid uses 2{,}000 validation and 8{,}000 test
examples. Performance on the CNN track is held-out test accuracy.

\subsection{The CNN $N{=}10$ Grid}
\label{app:cnn-grid}

Tables~\ref{tab:grid-retrain}--\ref{tab:grid-kendall} give all methods on the
stage where both Shapley values are computed exhaustively over $2^{10}$
subsets. The CIFAR-10 Dirichlet rows of Table~\ref{tab:grid-retrain} are
omitted because Table~1 of the main paper already reports them. The first
column is not a method but the value of the in-run Shapley itself against the
retrain SV, that is the agreement between the two Shapley values. The first
piece of news in this table is that the agreement differs from stage to
stage. By corruption mean, MNIST Dirichlet is highest at $+0.926$ and
CIFAR-10 IID lowest at $+0.661$, and the clean cell of CIFAR-10 IID reverses
sign to $-0.273$. That the non-IID cell of MNIST is above its IID cell
($+0.744$) says that the two games converge on the same answer when there
are real differences between clients and diverge otherwise. Flirds tracks
that agreement almost exactly in all three settings (corruption mean $+0.644$
to $+0.918$, at most $0.019$ away from the agreement), whereas scoring
against the in-run Shapley puts Flirds first in all 16 cells (with the
label-flip cell of MNIST Dirichlet tied at $1.000$ with Flirds-1st). In the
gradient-noise column of Table~\ref{tab:grid-inrun}, Flirds-1st alone drops
to $-0.188$ under CIFAR-10 Dirichlet, which means the verdict from the
partial-participation stage (Table~\ref{tab:mnist-spearman}) is reproduced
under full participation as well.

\section{Reproducibility of the Target Signal}
\label{app:signal}

The low correlations of the clean cell, both those of Section~5.2 of the main
paper and those of Supplementary Section~\ref{app:fidelity}, are not used as
evidence for or against any method. The grounds are in
this appendix. A correlation carries meaning only when the Shapley value it
scores against is a ranking that reproduces across seeds, and on a stage
where there are no real differences between clients that ranking itself
changes from seed to seed. Table~\ref{tab:target-stability} compares the
in-run Shapley directly between pairs of seeds, and on the stages that come
out low there, a high correlation is no more than an accurate reproduction of
a ranking that does not reproduce.

\section{Intervention: Full Tables}
\label{app:intervention}

Section~5.3 of the main paper reports the CIFAR-10 Dirichlet cell at the
selection-retrain point and the LLM main setting. This appendix holds the
rest: the online point, where the same policy is applied during deployment,
the repetition on MNIST and on the IID partition, and the removal curve,
which judges the contribution ranking directly from outside the game. The
online point keeps the gate off for the first 10 rounds, leaves any client
observed fewer than twice in the candidate pool, and reinstates one excluded
client in turn every 5 rounds so that exclusion does not become an absorbing
state. Rounds in which the candidate pool would be empty revert to full
participation.

The bottom arm of the tables is a separate run without any intervention,
configured identically to the observer run that supplies the scores. On
CIFAR-10 the two runs ride different random streams and part company in the
third decimal place, so the bottom arm was unified to the observer run in
order to read it and the cells where the gate excludes nobody, and where
retraining therefore coincides with training without intervention, off the
same run. On MNIST the final accuracies of the two runs are bit-identical in
all 18 pairs.

\subsection{Removal Curve}
\label{app:removal}

Table~\ref{tab:removal-cnn} judges the contribution ranking from outside the
game. The wider the gap between the curve obtained by retraining while
removing clients from the lowest rank up and the curve obtained by removing
them from the highest rank down, the more causal that ranking is for
performance. The in-run Shapley and Flirds produce essentially the same gap
under all three threats (a difference of at most $0.15$~points), and under
gradient noise Flirds-1st alone reverses sign, to $-1.89$~points. The
renormalizing family collapses in the free-rider cell (ShapleyFL at
$-16.26$~points). In the clean cell every method sits near zero, so the
removal order does not change performance, which is the same fact as the
absence of signal diagnosed in Supplementary Section~\ref{app:signal}.
